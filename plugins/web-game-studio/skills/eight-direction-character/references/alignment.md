# Compare candidates and measure the pivot

Compare the original and candidate pixels side by side at the same raw scale before aligning them. Check the whole-body direction, camera pitch, head-to-body proportions, pose, materials and equipment sides. Compare neighboring directions at the game's intended display size. Reject visible design or perspective errors that resizing cannot correct.

Once their members exist, review the front group SW–S–SE and the back group NW–N–NE together. Check the side transitions NE–E–SE and NW–W–SW the same way. For each group, use its first image as `path`, the next two as `characterComparePaths`, and the game height as `characterPreviewHeight`. Skip a group already reviewed together if none of its members changed. Recheck a corrected view with its two neighbors. In the playable preview, cycle the eight published views at a fixed foot anchor to spot any remaining jump.

The raster details for the original and managed results give the native width and height, clear/partial/opaque pixel counts, and bounds of nonzero alpha. The opaque count measures alpha exactly 255. A model can return a solid-looking interior at alpha 250–254. The receipt's `nearOpaquePixels` counts pixels at alpha 250 or higher. Judge any visible halo on contrasting backgrounds at game size. Bounds have inclusive left/top and exclusive right/bottom edges. They cover the entire visible silhouette, including hats, antennae, weapons and projecting toes. Pick the anatomical landmarks from the pixels. Use the controlled project image reader with just the saved path to open native pixels. Canvas-only inputs stay outside the isolated shell. The generation receipt already provides their numerical dimensions and alpha bounds. Measure at native dimensions with the top-left at (0,0). Use the original image’s framing for landmark coordinates. The two-background preview trims transparent margins and adds its own padding. Use the recorded y coordinates for the arithmetic directly in the publish arguments.

- `sourceHeadY` is the y coordinate of the anatomical crown. Identify it consistently even when a hat rises above it.
- `sourceGroundPoint:{x,y}` is the planted-foot contact anchor for movement. A near toe can project below it. Choose the same physical anchor across directions. For a level two-foot stance, locate each weight-bearing sole separately and use the midpoint between their ground contacts. A straight view with both visible soles floating above that midpoint needs a new measurement.
- Choose a shared output `headToGround`. For each view, `scale = headToGround / (sourceGroundPoint.y - sourceHeadY)`. For instance, crown y=24 and ground y=344 give a scale of 0.3 for a 96 px output height. Use `nearest` for pixel art or `lanczos3` for smooth art.
- Choose `canvas:{width,height}` and a shared destination `pivot:{x,y}` with enough padding around every transformed silhouette, including hair, bag and weapons. The host maps each point to `pivot + scale × (sourcePoint − sourceGroundPoint)`. It checks crown height within two display pixels and rejects clipped visible pixels.

Inspect the receipt’s `reviewRead` on light and dark backgrounds for visible residue and painted edges at game size. Preserve intentional soft alpha. Check the source image and its native dimensions for clipping and landmark coordinates. Open a playtest only after a runnable page exists. At the published scale, toggle straight and diagonal views on a stationary ground line. If both soles float above the line in a straight view or the body jumps on switching, correct the source ground measurement and republish the derivative.

For a local cleanup, retain the original and register the reviewed transparent derivative as a source view under a fresh view revision. Publish with measurements taken on that derivative.
