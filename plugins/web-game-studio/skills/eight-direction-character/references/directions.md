# Identify the reference and map the directions

Inspect the full character. A usable S faces straight toward the camera with head, chest, hips and feet aligned, neutral weight-bearing stance, correct camera pitch and the full silhouette visible. A face with one cheek or shoulder emphasized may be a three-quarter view. Compare the actual angle with the game or brief.

- If the front matches and the image has a clean transparent background, register it as S. The publisher accepts static PNG or WebP with actual alpha and produces PNG.
- If the front matches but has an opaque background, preserve the original. Reuse a verified transparent cutout if one exists, or make a separate clean derivative when feasible. Otherwise generate a transparent S. A JPEG or painted checkerboard needs this step too.
- If the reference is angled, cropped or at the wrong pitch, keep it for identity and prepare a true S first. A qualifying angled source can also fill its own direction. On a focused revision, reuse the set's accepted baseline.

Record the identifying silhouette, proportions, colors, stance, camera and any asymmetric equipment. In screen coordinates X increases rightward and Y downward.

| Direction | What the viewer sees |
| --- | --- |
| S | Straight front, facing down |
| SE / SW | Front three-quarter, facing down-right / down-left |
| E / W | Profile, facing right / left |
| NE / NW | Rear three-quarter, facing up-right / up-left |
| N | Straight rear, facing up |

Rotate the whole body in 45-degree steps with the camera fixed. Use the character's anatomical left and right. A left hip bag is on screen right in S, screen left in N, on the far side in E and near side in W. Allow natural occlusion. Follow fixed off-center details through a half turn using their physical location on the character. Check that a fastener or mark moves to the opposite screen side when its projection should reverse. Generate asymmetric views independently. Only use a mirror for a confirmed symmetric design with the user's agreement.

With only front art, infer a coherent back from visible straps, clothing and equipment. Before using N as a reference, trace each strap across S and N. A chest buckle or badge should rotate out of sight on the rear view. If matching hardware appears on both sides, check the reference or design brief for a second attachment. Check the same detail where it becomes visible or occluded in each adjacent diagonal. Carry the reviewed rear design into those diagonals. Ask about an unseen detail only if the brief leaves materially different choices that you cannot resolve.
